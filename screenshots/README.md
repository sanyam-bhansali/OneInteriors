# One Interiors — every screen

Four folders, in the order a person meets them. Each has `desktop/` and
`mobile/`; the numbers are the order you would walk through, not the order
the files happen to sort in.

> Generated by `npm run shots`. Re-run it after any UI change rather than
> editing anything here — this file is overwritten.

## 00-entry · Getting in

The pages anyone can reach: the marketplace front door, the two sign-in paths, and the form a studio applies through. Everything else in this folder set sits behind one of these.

- **01 The marketplace front door** — The pitch: nine questions, studios that actually fit, a real quote from each, and an architect who checks every step. Scroll-driven — the how-it-works section pins a list of steps beside real product views rather than generic illustrations.
- **02 Every studio on the roster** — The full list, with what each has been verified on. Deliberately not ranked by anything a studio can pay for.
- **03 One studio, in public** — Their work, their range, their localities, and the twelve checks with the state of each. The page a customer reads before deciding.
- **04 What "verified" actually means here** — The twelve checks, spelled out, including what each one does NOT prove. The page that makes the badge mean something.
- **05 Sign in — customer** — Name and mobile, then a code on WhatsApp. No password: a customer signs in rarely, and a forgotten password is one more wall between them and their quotes.
- **06 Sign in — studio and ops** — Email and password first, because staff sign in to work and do it often. The emailed link sits directly underneath, never hidden, for a forgotten password or a first visit.
- **07 That link has expired** — Explains why rather than just failing: links last fifteen minutes and work once, and this page says so and offers another.
- **08 Choose a password** — Where a studio lands the first time they redeem their approval link. Thirty seconds, at the one moment we know they are reading something from us. Skippable. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **09 A studio applies** — The form a practice fills in to be considered. Asks for the things that decide it — years working, localities, range, what they have finished — and nothing else.

## 01-customer · The customer journey

A homeowner in Pune, from "I want to do up my flat" to holding three comparable quotes and an architect who has read them. Hidden behind the waitlist in production today — CUSTOMER_LIVE opens it.

- **01 Nine questions** — The brief being written, a question at a time. Shows the answers accumulating and a live count of how many studios still match — so the customer sees their choices narrowing the field rather than filling a form.
- **02 What this is likely to cost** — An honest range from the brief, with what moves it up and down. Before any studio is involved, so the number is not anchored by whoever quoted first.
- **03 Who fits, and why** — Studios scored against the brief, each card showing the reason it matched and work that backs it. Cards open on scroll; verification ticks appear beside the projects in glass frames. Nobody can pay to sit higher, and the page says so. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **04 The quotes arrive** — Generated from each studio's own rate card in about three seconds. No studio is asked, nobody is phoned. Line items carry quantity and spec, not a single lump sum. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **05 Side by side, and the materials behind them** — The quotes next to each other AND what each is actually made of — carcass, shutter, hardware. Tap any material to see what it means. This is where a cheaper quote stops looking cheaper.
- **06 Talk to an architect** — Requesting the person who reads the quotes with them. Named, with their background — not a call centre.
- **07 Before the call** — Three things to have ready: the floor plan, the rooms that matter, and what has already been decided. So the call starts at the real question. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **08 Their account** — Their brief, their quotes, their consultations, and what we hold about them. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **09 A quote shared with someone else** — The read-only view when a customer sends a quote to a partner or parent. No account needed, and the link can be revoked. _(did not capture — see below)_

## 02-studio · The studio practice software

What a design studio gets: onboarding onto the roster, then the software they run their practice in — leads, projects, quotations, rates, their own public listing.

- **01 Onboarding 1 of 5 — profile** — How they describe themselves, and where they work. The words a customer reads first. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **02 Onboarding 2 of 5 — registration** — GSTIN and registration numbers, checked against public records. Has an escape hatch — a practice without a GSTIN is not turned away at the door. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **03 Onboarding 3 of 5 — portfolio** — Three completed projects. This is what customers actually read, and it is the step with no escape hatch — a genuinely new practice stops here. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **04 Onboarding 4 of 5 — rates** — Their rate card. Private, never shown to anyone but them, and the thing every quote is generated from. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **05 Onboarding 5 of 5 — review** — Send it to us. Then we check the registration, ring two past clients, and visit two finished sites. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **06 The studio dashboard** — What needs doing today — leads waiting, quotations to send, where each project stands. The morning screen. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **07 Leads and clients** — Every enquiry on a board with columns the studio defines themselves. A brand-new studio finds one sample lead here, clearly marked, removable, and excluded from every count. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **08 Bring your existing clients** — CSV import, so a studio joining does not start from an empty board with a spreadsheet open beside it. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **09 The bin** — Deleted clients, restorable for 30 days. Deleting a lead by accident should not be permanent. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **10 Projects** — Work that has been won, and where each one stands. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **11 The work pool** — Tasks across projects and who they are assigned to. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **12 Quotations** — Every quotation the studio has raised, and its state. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **13 The product master** — What they build and what it costs them. New studios start from a neutral starter set rather than a blank table. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **14 Rates** — The rate card every generated quote comes from. Theirs to change, and private. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **15 Vendors** — Who they buy from, and on what terms. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **16 Calendar** — Site visits, client meetings, and the consultations we have introduced. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **17 Their profile** — Editing what customers read. Every word is theirs to approve before it goes live. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **18 How they appear to customers** — Their own listing as a customer sees it, plus where they stand on each of the twelve checks. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **19 Settings — branding** — Their logo and colours, which go onto the quotation PDF. The quote a client receives looks like the studio sent it, because they did. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **20 Settings — pipeline** — The columns on their board, named and coloured by them. Every practice runs differently and the software should not argue. _(did not capture — see below)_
- **21 Settings — custom fields** — Extra fields on a client record, for whatever this studio tracks that we did not think of. _(did not capture — see below)_

## 03-ops · The ops console

Yours. Who applied, who has been verified and how, which customer went to which studio, and what happened next.

- **01 The console** — What needs your attention: applications waiting, verifications in progress, consultations to staff.
- **02 Who has applied** — Every application, with a button that reads their website and shows what it claims — labelled as claims, never as evidence. Approve creates the studio and emails them a sign-in link; reject requires a reason.
- **03 Verification** — The twelve checks per studio and where each one stands. What has been proven, by whom, and when.
- **04 One studio, everything we know** — Legal name, GSTIN, our own private assessment, their history with us. Not shown to anyone else, ever.
- **05 Allocation** — How many briefs each studio is seeing, and the controls that shape it.
- **06 Introductions** — Which customer was introduced to which studio, when, and what came of it.
- **07 Consultations** — Architect calls requested and booked, each with the prep pack the customer filled in.
- **08 The funnel** — Where people arrive, where they stop, and how many get through each step.
- **09 Export** — Getting the data out in a shape you can think in, rather than reading it through a dashboard somebody else designed.
