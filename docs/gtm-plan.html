The waitlist is how you buy supply.

Five days to 16 September. What can honestly be promised on that date, where the first two hundred briefs come from when the obvious list is legally blocked, and the one number the first month exists to measure.

The premise

You are not launching a marketplace. You are collecting the evidence that lets you sign studios.

On 16 September the product cannot produce a quote. Not because of a bug — because no studio has entered a rate card, and the pricing code refuses to invent one. That refusal is correct and it is the whole trust proposition, but it means the headline promise is six weeks away from being deliverable.

So invert the usual order. A waitlist of Pune homeowners who have each told you their flat, their budget band, the styles they want and the ones they have ruled out is not a consolation prize while you wait for supply — it is the single most persuasive thing you can put in front of a studio. "Join our platform" is a weak cold open. "We have 140 briefs in Baner and Kharadi, here is the budget distribution, do you want to be one of the first ten studios to see them" is a different conversation entirely.

What the customer actually gets on day one

The nine-question brief, the style reveal, and what their own flat costs at three levels of finish — all of which work today, with zero studios, because those bands are ours rather than any studio's. That is a real answer to the question they came with, and it is honest about what comes next: a place in the queue, not a quote.

The promise to avoid on 16 September

Anything of the form "get real quotes from verified Pune studios." It is true in November and false in September, and a waitlist that goes quiet for six weeks after an unfulfilled promise is worse than no waitlist. Six weeks of silence turns a warm list cold and the second email gets marked as spam.

Supply

Ten studios, approached in four waves, easiest first

The financial model needs ten studios live with a project each in month one. Work outward from the warmest relationship, because each wave's yes makes the next wave's pitch stronger — by wave three you are not describing a plan, you are naming studios who already said yes.

The agency-client conversation, said plainly

Twenty interior designers currently pay your agency for ads. Turning them into platform tenants is the obvious move and the one most likely to go wrong quietly, because from their side it can look like their marketing partner has started competing for their customers.

Say it first, in one sentence: this is a different product, your ad spend with us is unaffected, and you pay nothing unless a project closes. The pilot terms make that easy — 5% on closed projects, no subscription for three months. There is no downside for them to say yes to, which is exactly why the pilot is priced that way.

Do not promise volume yet

The subscription tiers imply 8 to 30 briefs a month. Nothing can deliver that in October, and a guarantee missed in month two is a churn event in a city where forty designers know each other. During the pilot promise one thing only: briefs that match, with the reasoning shown — and be specific about how many are in the list today.

The first ₹25,000 is an instrument, not a campaign

The entire financial model rests on one unverified pair of numbers: ₹200 a lead at a 10% close rate, which implies ₹2,000 to acquire a project worth ₹12.75 lakh. The kill-criteria in the build plan imply a figure twelve times higher. Both cannot be right.

So spend the first ₹25k with one job: measure cost per completed brief, and what share of those briefs sit in the ₹7 lakh-plus band. Everything else in the plan — the studio count, the subscription price, whether year one works at all — is downstream of that number. Getting it in week one costs almost nothing; getting it in month four costs the year.

The first honest decision point

  • Gate: 150+ completed briefs, 4+ studios with rates in, cost per completed brief known and under ₹2,500, and brief completion above 40%.

Measurement

Four numbers, and what each one would make you do

All four are already instrumented — the funnel view in the ops console answers the first two today, and it does so from our own tables with no third-party script on the site.

NumberWatch forWhat it would mean
Brief completion < 40% The nine questions are too long, or the value is not landing before the ask. This is a product fix and a cheap one — the funnel view names the exact question losing people.
Cost per completed brief > ₹2,500 No viable paid channel. The answer is owned demand — societies, builders, referral — not a bigger budget. This is the year-defining number.
Briefs in the ₹7L+ band < 50% The channel is bringing the wrong people. Cheap to fix by targeting, expensive to ignore, because a studio shown four unqualified briefs concludes the platform does not work.
Studio conversation → signed < 1 in 4 The offer is wrong, not the pitch. Most likely the pilot terms are not as risk-free as they read, or the volume promise is not credible.

Blocking

Three decisions, none of them engineering

Blocks 16 September

The name. Domain, Instagram handle, the noindex flag and every piece of outreach copy wait on it. It has been deferred since v0.3 for good reasons, and it has now become the thing standing between you and a launch date. Decide it this week even if the answer is imperfect.

Blocks the demand plan

DPDP on the 20,000 records. Until answered, the largest demand source is unusable and the whole first-quarter plan runs on channels that are slower and smaller. A two-hour question that has been open for a week.

Blocks year two, not year one

Escrow. A fifth of year-two revenue rests on holding client funds, which needs an RBI-compliant partner nobody has confirmed we can have. Not urgent for 16 September — overdue for the plan the business is being built against.

Answerable this week, for free

Will studios accept milestone-gated payment? The load-bearing assumption of the whole model and the one most likely to be false. Hauspire and Urbanline are both in the queue and one of them is a cofounder's business. Ask both, plainly, before anyone builds escrow.

Written against build plan v0.6 and the two-year model of the same date. Figures for studio counts, project values and channel yields are the model's assumptions rather than measurements — the first month exists to replace them with real numbers, starting with cost per completed brief.
