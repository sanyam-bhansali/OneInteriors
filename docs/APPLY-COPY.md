# /apply — full copy draft

Two pages. **`/apply`** is the pitch and ends in one button. **`/apply/start`**
is the form. Nobody should be asked to decide and to type at the same time.

Design language is the locked one — Raw Silk ground, Alabaster cards, Deep
Espresso type, Terracotta for the one action that matters, Muted Sage for
verification marks. Mono eyebrow → serif heading → sans body. Squared corners
except on glass.

## 4 · What you get

> **Eyebrow:** WHAT YOU GET
>
> **H2 (serif):** Four things you do not get anywhere else.

**A customer who has already chosen you.**
They have read your profile, looked at your finished work, and compared your
quote against two others. The first conversation starts at "when can you come"
rather than "what do you charge".

**A site visit, not a phone number.**
Every introduction carries the flat, the configuration, the carpet area and a
time they are free. You walk in knowing the space.

**Your pricing already seen, and accepted.**
They saw your numbers before they asked for you. Nobody is going to ask you to
cut thirty percent after the measurement.

**The software to run the rest of your practice.**
Your leads, your projects, your quotations priced off your own rate card, and a
PDF that goes out with your logo on it — not ours. Yours whether we send you
work that month or not.

**Visual.** Four cards, two by two on desktop, stacked on mobile. Alabaster,
squared, hairline border. Each one gets a small mono numeral in the corner —
01 to 04 — in the terracotta ink. No icons: an icon of "a customer who has
already chosen you" is always a cliché, and four clichés in a row reads as a
template.

# What was removed, and why

The three-column strip — *No commission on your existing clients* / *We publish
the bad numbers too* / *We do not hold your money* — is gone from the body.

All three are true and two of them are good policy. They read as answers to
questions the studio has not asked yet, and they are phrased as our rules
rather than their gain. "We do not hold your money" invites the thought *why
would you hold my money?*

What survives, and where:

- **The commission point** is a benefit, so it goes in the footer line as one
  plain sentence. A studio will ask about money; leaving it unanswered on the
  page it is decided on is worse than a quiet line.
- **Publishing the bad numbers** becomes the second bullet of the honest
  filter, where it does real work — as a reason not to apply if you quote low.
- **Not holding money** belongs in the FAQ or the first call, not here.

# Open questions for you

1. **Commission rate.** The footer says "we charge only on work we bring you".
   Should the actual percentage be on this page? Naming it is the single
   strongest trust move available, and every studio will ask in the first call
   anyway.
2. **"Eight studios"** is a fixture count today. Compute it, or write the
   sentence without a number.
3. **The photograph** in the hero is licensed stock standing in for real work.
   Fine for now; it must be a real project before this page is shown to anyone.
